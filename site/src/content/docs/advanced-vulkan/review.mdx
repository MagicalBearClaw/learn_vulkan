Part 3 ended with a renderer that loaded real models and bound nothing per draw. It drew
them with the fixed-function state 1.13 had set up once and never questioned: `LESS`
against a buffer cleared to 1.0, back faces culled, no blending, one render target, and a
descriptor for every buffer a shader read.

Part 4 so far has taken each of those apart. This page reviews chapters 4.1 to 4.5; it will
grow as the rest of the part — instancing and compute among it — is written.

This chapter has no new code.

## What you built

| Chapter | Added |

## The frame, assembled

4.5's frame is every chapter of the part in one command buffer:

```
   on_render
      │
      ├── arena.reset()                                            ── 4.5
      ├── write 7 × Globals, 10 × DrawData  ──▶ 2 952 bytes, 9 addresses
      │
      ├── barrier: live cube ──▶ COLOR_ATTACHMENT_OPTIMAL (6 layers)  ── 4.3 / 4.4
      ├── for each of 6 faces                                      ── 4.4
      │      barrier: face depth, write ──▶ next clear   (after face 0)
      │      begin_rendering(face view, face depth)
      │      floor, crates      cull BACK, front = CLOCKWISE       ── 4.2 / 4.4
      │      ferns              cull NONE, discard                 ── 4.2
      │      sky                LESS_OR_EQUAL, xyww, cull FRONT    ── 4.1 / 4.4
      ├── barrier: live cube ──▶ SHADER_READ_ONLY_OPTIMAL
      │
      └── screen pass
             floor, crates, ferns   front = COUNTER_CLOCKWISE
             sphere                 reflect(incident, n) into the live cube
             sky                    drawn last, rejected wherever the scene is
```

Every draw in it pushes 32 bytes — three addresses and an index — and binds exactly one
descriptor set, for its texture.

## The mathematics Part 4 derived

Each result was derived where it was first needed, and each was checked against the sample
rather than asserted.

**Depth precision falls with the square of distance** (4.1). The window-space depth is
`z_w = f/(f−n) · (1 − n/d)`, so `dz_w/dd = f n / ((f−n) d²)`, and the smallest separation the
buffer can resolve is `Δd = Δz_w · (f−n) d² / (f n)`. With `n = 0.1`, `f = 100`: 0.6 µm at 1 m,
1.49 mm at 50 m, half of every representable value spent in the first ten centimetres.
Reversed-Z brings 50 m down to 2.9 µm — and needs the projection changed too, not just the
compare op.

**The stencil test is one integer comparison, reference on the left** (4.1):
`(reference & compareMask) compareOp (stencil & compareMask)`. Measured, not assumed: with
`reference = 1`, `GREATER` renders byte-identical to `NOT_EQUAL` and `LESS` draws nothing.

**Winding is the sign of a determinant** (4.2, again in 4.4). A screen triangle's signed area
is `½·det[b − a, c − a]`, and a linear map multiplies every signed area by its own determinant.
The y-flip has determinant −1, so it reverses every winding — which is why `frontFace` and the
projection are one decision. A cube face's `(s, t, forward)` frame has determinant −1 too, which
is why 4.4's face pipelines are `CLOCKWISE`.

**Blending order has an exact error** (4.2). Swapping two layers of the over operator changes
the result by `a_A · a_B · (C_A − C_B)`, independent of the background. All 18 055 pixels that
change when 4.2's sort is turned off match that to within 0.011.

**Alpha composes as coverage** (4.2): `a_out = 1 − (1 − a_src)(1 − a_dst) = a_src + a_dst(1 − a_src)`.
The obvious `ONE, ZERO` stores `a_src` instead, and predicts the exact byte — 140 — that the
reference capture found.

**A kernel's sum is what it does to a flat region** (4.3). Sum 1 keeps brightness, sum 0 keeps
only variation. The 8-neighbour edge kernel is `3∇²f` by Taylor expansion, so a smooth ramp
comes out black, and sharpen is exactly `identity − edges`.

**Face selection picks the major axis** (4.4), and one face covers the directions where the
other two components are no larger than it — a 45° half-angle, so a 90° camera. The
specification's `sc`/`tc` table fixes every face camera's right and up vectors; all twelve
were checked.

**Refraction follows Snell's law, and entering glass cannot totally reflect** (4.4).
`k = 1 − η²(1 − cos² θ) ≥ 1 − η² > 0` whenever `η < 1`, so the sample's guard never fires. Schlick
gives `F₀ = ((n₁ − n₂)/(n₁ + n₂))²` — 0.043 for glass, the 0.04 in the shader.

**Rounding up to a power of two is a mask** (4.5): `(x + a − 1) & ~(a − 1)`.

## The Vulkan lessons

**Pipeline state changes the picture as much as shaders do.** 4.1's outline and 4.2's glass are
drawn by fragment shaders one line long. Depth test, depth write, compare op, bias, stencil,
cull mode, front face and blend state are all fixed when the pipeline is built, which is why
4.1 has five pipelines, 4.2 seven, and 4.4 two of everything.

**A barrier is two dependencies.** Execution — the source stages finish before the destination
stages start — and memory — the source writes are made available and then visible. 4.3's
missing-barrier experiment showed the difference: the frames were still correctly *ordered* by
vkcommon's frame-start barrier, and synchronization validation still reported a hazard,
because nothing had made the writes visible.

**Addresses for buffers, descriptors for images.** Descriptor sets across the part:

| | per-frame data reached by | descriptor sets | set layouts | push constant |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | uniform buffer | 4 | 2 | 128 B |
| 4.4 | 14 uniform buffers | 19 | 2 | 128 B |
| 4.5 | one arena, by address | 5 | 1 | 32 B |

The five that remain in 4.5 are all textures. An image is bytes *plus* format, tiling, swizzle
and filtering, and no 64-bit number can carry that.

## Every barrier in Part 4

| Where | Layout | Source stage / access | Destination stage / access | Hazard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.3, into the scene image | `UNDEFINED` → `COLOR_ATTACHMENT_OPTIMAL` | fragment shader / sampled read | colour output / colour write | write after read, across frames |
| 4.3, out of the scene image | `COLOR_ATTACHMENT_OPTIMAL` → `SHADER_READ_ONLY_OPTIMAL` | colour output / colour write | fragment shader / sampled read | read after write |
| 4.4, into the live cube | same as 4.3's first, all six layers | | | write after read |
| 4.4, between faces | depth, unchanged | late fragment tests / depth write | early fragment tests / depth write | write after write |
| 4.4, out of the live cube | same as 4.3's second, all six layers | | | read after write |

The two 4.3 barriers chain across frames through the fragment-shader stage they share, which
is how the second one also protects the first frame's writes from the next frame's layout
transition.

## What is in `vkcommon` now

**Nothing new, for the third part running.** Every file in `code/common/` is still owned by a
chapter in Part 1. What Part 4 did instead was be the first to *call* things Part 1 had written
and set aside for it:

| Written in | Used first in |
|---|---|
| 1.3 — `shaderDemoteToHelperInvocation` | 4.2, the first `discard` |
| 1.3 — `bufferDeviceAddress`, `scalarBlockLayout` | 4.5, the first pointer |
| 1.7 — `depth_bias`, `stencil_attachment`, `stencil_test` | 4.1 |
| 1.7 — `colour_blend`, `alpha_blending` | 4.2 |
| 1.7 — `vkc::image_barrier` | 4.3 |
| 1.11 — `ImageDesc::array_layers`, `cube`, `vkc::load_cubemap` | 4.4 |

The samples call those rather than carrying their own copies. 4.3 and 4.4 used to define local
versions of `image_barrier` and of cubemap creation and loading; they now use the shared code,
and render byte-identically for it — 4.4's static-sky round trip still reads zero pixels
different.

What stays in the chapters is what only they need: 4.1's depth-stencil buffer and three-attachment
`begin_rendering`, 4.4's six per-face views, and 4.5's `buffer_address` and `FrameArena`.

## Things Part 4 does badly on purpose

**Transparency is sorted per object.** 4.2 sorts panes by centre distance. Two panes that
intersect cannot be ordered that way at all, and order-independent transparency is a later
subject.

**The environment map is right at one point.** 4.4 renders it from the sphere's centre, every
frame, at 512 per face — seven scene draws where there was one. Real renderers update such maps
rarely, small, and only near the camera.

**The sphere has one surface.** Light refracts in and never out, so total internal reflection
never happens and the glass has no back. And one `F₀` serves water, glass and diamond.

**Nothing checks an address.** 4.5 reads through raw pointers with no bounds checks and no
validation message for a stale one; GPU-assisted validation is the tool for that, and it has not
been needed yet.

**The shaders repeat themselves.** 4.5's two shaders declare the same four structs word for word,
and the C++ declares them a third time. With descriptors gone, those declarations are the only
contract between the two sides — which is the problem 4.6 starts on.

## Before moving on

One comparison is worth making yourself:

```bash
diff code/src/4.advanced_vulkan/cubemaps/main.cpp \
     code/src/4.advanced_vulkan/buffer_device_address/main.cpp
```

The pictures differ in one pixel by one level. The diff is most of the file. That is the part's
last lesson in one command: the same frame can be delivered by very different plumbing, and a
pixel comparison against a known-good image is how you know the new plumbing is right.
