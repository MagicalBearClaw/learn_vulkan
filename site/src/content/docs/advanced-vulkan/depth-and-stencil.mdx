Every chapter since 1.7 changed the picture by changing what a shader computed. This one
does not. Its fragment shaders are the shortest in the series so far — one lit surface,
one flat colour — and the orange ring around each crate is not drawn by either of them.

What changes the picture here is the state the fragments meet *after* the fragment shader
has finished: the depth test, and the stencil test sitting next to it in the same struct.

## The struct that has been there all along

`VkPipelineDepthStencilStateCreateInfo` has been in `PipelineBuilder` since 1.13, filled
in and never discussed:

```cpp title="code/common/scaffold/src/pipeline.cpp"
const VkPipelineDepthStencilStateCreateInfo depth_stencil{
    .depthTestEnable = depth_test_ ? VK_TRUE : VK_FALSE,
    .depthWriteEnable = depth_write_ ? VK_TRUE : VK_FALSE,
    .depthCompareOp = depth_compare_,
    .depthBoundsTestEnable = VK_FALSE,
    .stencilTestEnable = stencil_test_ ? VK_TRUE : VK_FALSE,
    .front = stencil_front_,
    .back = stencil_back_,
    .minDepthBounds = 0.0F,
    .maxDepthBounds = 1.0F,
};
```

Three of those fields are worth separating, because they are routinely confused:

- **`depthTestEnable`** decides whether the fragment's depth is *compared* at all. Off
  means every fragment survives, whatever is in front of it.
- **`depthWriteEnable`** decides whether a surviving fragment's depth is *stored*. Off
  means the surface is visible but invisible to everything drawn after it — which is
  exactly what a transparent surface wants, and what 4.2 will need.
- **`depthCompareOp`** decides what "closer" means. `LESS` with a buffer cleared to 1.0 is
  the arrangement this series has used throughout, and it is a convention, not a law.

The outline pipeline in this chapter turns the first two off together. An outline is a
decoration in screen space, not a thing in the world: it should not be buried by the floor
it overlaps, and nothing drawn later should be occluded by it.

## Two surfaces in one plane

The slab in the picture is a quad lying at exactly the floor's y. Both surfaces produce a
depth value at every pixel they share, the two values are the same number computed along
different paths, and `LESS` has to pick one.

The textbook answer is that they z-fight — a shimmering speckle of one surface and the
other. **That is not what happened here, and it is worth saying so.** With the slab as a
smaller quad with its own vertices, it simply won, everywhere, on this GPU, with no help
at all. Turning depth bias on and off changes 333 pixels out of 921600 — 0.04%, and all of
them on the slab's own edges.

That is luck, not a guarantee, and the way to see what the depth test really does with a
tie is to remove the luck: give the slab the floor's exact vertices, so the two depth
values are computed by the same arithmetic from the same inputs and come out bit for bit
identical.

<figure>
  <img src="/img/4.1/coplanar-unbiased.png" alt="The grid floor with no slab visible at all" />
  <figcaption>Identical coplanar geometry, no depth bias. The slab is drawn, and every one of its fragments is rejected.</figcaption>
</figure>

With `VK_COMPARE_OP_LESS`, equal is not less. The slab loses every pixel and disappears
completely — not a speckle, not a fight, an absence. Switch the bias on and it is the
floor that vanishes instead:

<figure>
  <img src="/img/4.1/coplanar-biased.png" alt="The same view with the painted slab covering the entire floor" />
  <figcaption>The same geometry with `depthBiasConstantFactor = -2.0`. 74.38% of the frame changes.</figcaption>
</figure>

Depth bias is the fixed-function answer to coplanar geometry, and it lives in the
rasterisation state rather than the depth-stencil state:

```cpp title="code/common/scaffold/src/pipeline.cpp"
.depthBiasEnable = depth_bias_ ? VK_TRUE : VK_FALSE,
.depthBiasConstantFactor = depth_bias_constant_,
.depthBiasClamp = 0.0F,
.depthBiasSlopeFactor = depth_bias_slope_,
```

The constant factor is not a distance. It is a multiple of *r*, the smallest difference the
depth format can resolve — a number the implementation defines, and the reason the bias is
expressed this way rather than in world units: the same offset has to work in a format
with 24 fixed-point bits and one with a float exponent.

The slope factor is scaled by how fast depth changes across the polygon. A floor seen at a
glancing angle covers an enormous depth range in very few pixels, and a constant offset
that is ample head-on is nothing at all out near the horizon.

:::note[Why the number is negative]
`LESS` means smaller wins, and Vulkan's depth range runs 0 at the near plane to 1 at the
far one. A negative bias moves a fragment toward the viewer, which is the direction that
wins the comparison. Under a reversed-Z setup — `GREATER` with the buffer cleared to 0 —
the sign flips with it.
:::

## The stencil test needs a format that has one

Everything so far was depth. The stencil test needs something the previous eleven chapters
never asked for: somewhere to put the stencil.

vkcommon's chooser, written in 1.13, asks for the most precise depth format first:

```cpp title="code/common/scaffold/src/image.cpp"
constexpr std::array candidates{
    VK_FORMAT_D32_SFLOAT,
    VK_FORMAT_D32_SFLOAT_S8_UINT,
    VK_FORMAT_D24_UNORM_S8_UINT,
};
```

`VK_FORMAT_D32_SFLOAT` has no stencil aspect at all, and it is the one that wins on any
sane desktop GPU. So this chapter writes its own chooser that considers only the two
combined formats. Vulkan guarantees at least one of them is supported as a depth-stencil
attachment, but not which, so both have to be asked for — on this machine (an RTX 4080) it
is `D32_SFLOAT_S8_UINT`.

Keeping 1.13's chooser and asking for a stencil anyway is instructive, because the
validation layers catch it four separate times before a single pixel is drawn:

```
vkCreateImageView(): pCreateInfo Using format (VK_FORMAT_D32_SFLOAT) with aspect flags
(VK_IMAGE_ASPECT_DEPTH_BIT|VK_IMAGE_ASPECT_STENCIL_BIT) but depth-only image formats can
have only the VK_IMAGE_ASPECT_DEPTH_BIT set.

vkCreateGraphicsPipelines(): pCreateInfos[0].pNext<VkPipelineRenderingCreateInfo>
.stencilAttachmentFormat (VK_FORMAT_D32_SFLOAT) does not have a stencil aspect
(need to use a stencil format).

vkCmdBeginRendering(): pRenderingInfo->pStencilAttachment->imageView was created with a
format (VK_FORMAT_D32_SFLOAT) that does not have a stencil aspect.
```

The aspect mask is the part that is easy to forget, because it is not one decision but
three that must agree: the view's `subresourceRange`, every barrier's `subresourceRange`,
and the layout. A depth-stencil image uses `VK_IMAGE_ASPECT_DEPTH_BIT |
VK_IMAGE_ASPECT_STENCIL_BIT` and `VK_IMAGE_LAYOUT_DEPTH_STENCIL_ATTACHMENT_OPTIMAL`, not
the depth-only versions 1.13 used.

## Three attachments, two of them the same view

Dynamic rendering describes depth and stencil separately, so `VkRenderingInfo` grows a
third attachment:

```cpp title="code/src/4.advanced_vulkan/depth_and_stencil/main.cpp"
const VkRenderingInfo rendering{
    .colorAttachmentCount = 1,
    .pColorAttachments = &colour_attachment,
    .pDepthAttachment = &depth_attachment,
    .pStencilAttachment = &stencil_attachment,
};
```

`depth_attachment` and `stencil_attachment` name **the same `VkImageView`**. That is not a
duplicate and not a mistake: a combined format is one image carrying two aspects, and they
are listed separately because they are cleared, loaded and stored separately. Here both
clear on load — depth to 1.0, stencil to 0 — and both are `DONT_CARE` on store, because
nothing outlives the frame.

Clearing the stencil is load-bearing. The outline pass asks "did a crate write here?", and
that question only means anything if the buffer started the frame saying no.

## Declaring the attachment is not enabling the test

Every pipeline in this chapter declares the stencil format, including the three that never
test the stencil:

```cpp title="code/src/4.advanced_vulkan/depth_and_stencil/main.cpp"
const auto base = [&]() -> vkc::PipelineBuilder {
    return vkc::PipelineBuilder(device)
        .shaders(shader)
        .vertex_input(std::span(&binding, 1), attributes)
        .colour_attachment(swapchain().format())
        .stencil_attachment(depth_stencil_format_)
        .layout(pipeline_layout_);
};
```

Without a render pass object, a pipeline carries its own description of the attachments it
expects, and that description has to match the rendering instance it is used inside.
Dropping `stencil_attachment()` and running gives:

```
vkCmdDrawIndexed(): VkRenderingInfo::pStencilAttachment->imageView format
(VK_FORMAT_D32_SFLOAT_S8_UINT) must match the corresponding format in
VkPipelineRenderingCreateInfo::stencilAttachmentFormat (VK_FORMAT_UNDEFINED).
```

Note where it lands: at the **draw**, not at pipeline creation. Nothing is wrong with
either object on its own; they are only incompatible once used together, which is the
characteristic shape of a dynamic-rendering mistake and worth recognising.

## Stamping a silhouette

The crates are drawn with the only state that differs from the floor's:

```cpp title="code/src/4.advanced_vulkan/depth_and_stencil/main.cpp"
const VkStencilOpState write_one{
    .failOp = VK_STENCIL_OP_KEEP,
    .passOp = VK_STENCIL_OP_REPLACE,
    .depthFailOp = VK_STENCIL_OP_KEEP,
    .compareOp = VK_COMPARE_OP_ALWAYS,
    .compareMask = 0xFF,
    .writeMask = 0xFF,
    .reference = 1,
};
```

`compareOp = ALWAYS` means this pass never rejects anything — it is writing, not reading.
`passOp = REPLACE` stores `reference` wherever the fragment also survived the depth test,
so what accumulates is the crates' *visible* silhouette, occlusions included.

`depthFailOp = KEEP` is the field that earns its place. A fragment that passes the stencil
test and fails the depth test is behind something else, and keeping the stencil unchanged
there is what stops a crate hidden behind a wall from stamping a silhouette that would then
be outlined straight through the wall.

The three op fields cover the three possible fates of a fragment, and every one of them
needs an answer: stencil failed, stencil passed but depth failed, both passed.

## Drawing only where nothing was

The outline is the same cube, 6% larger, with the test inverted:

```cpp title="code/src/4.advanced_vulkan/depth_and_stencil/main.cpp"
const VkStencilOpState keep_where_unstamped{
    .failOp = VK_STENCIL_OP_KEEP,
    .passOp = VK_STENCIL_OP_KEEP,
    .depthFailOp = VK_STENCIL_OP_KEEP,
    .compareOp = VK_COMPARE_OP_NOT_EQUAL,
    .compareMask = 0xFF,
    .writeMask = 0x00,
    .reference = 1,
};
```

`NOT_EQUAL 1` keeps only fragments landing where no crate was drawn, which of a
slightly-enlarged crate is precisely the rim protruding past the real one. `writeMask = 0`
because this pass reads the stencil and must leave it intact for the next crate.

The draw order matters more than any of the state does:

```cpp title="code/src/4.advanced_vulkan/depth_and_stencil/main.cpp"
vkCmdBindPipeline(frame.cmd, VK_PIPELINE_BIND_POINT_GRAPHICS, crate_pipeline_);
for (const CratePlacement& crate : kCrates) { /* ... */ }

if (outlines_) {
    vkCmdBindPipeline(frame.cmd, VK_PIPELINE_BIND_POINT_GRAPHICS, outline_pipeline_);
    for (const CratePlacement& crate : kCrates) { /* ... */ }
}
```

Every crate stamps its silhouette before any outline is drawn. Interleave the two — crate,
outline, crate, outline — and the first crate's halo spills across the second crate, which
has not yet written the stencil that would have masked it. All the writes, then all the
reads.

:::note[What moved into vkcommon]
Three setters on `PipelineBuilder`, all of them pass-throughs:

```cpp title="code/common/scaffold/include/vkc/pipeline.hpp"
PipelineBuilder& depth_bias(float constant_factor, float slope_factor);
PipelineBuilder& stencil_attachment(VkFormat format);
PipelineBuilder& stencil_test(VkStencilOpState front, VkStencilOpState back);
```

The two `VkStencilOpState` structs are still written out field by field in this chapter's
`main.cpp`, as 1.9 did with its specialisation entries. The builder carries them; it does
not decide anything.

The depth-stencil *buffer* stayed here rather than moving. vkcommon's
`create_depth_buffer()` is 1.13's, produces a depth-only image, and every chapter from 1.13
to 3.4 still wants exactly that.
:::

## What changed since 3.4

- a depth-stencil format, chosen from the two combined candidates rather than 1.13's list
- an image whose aspect mask, view and layout all name depth *and* stencil
- a third attachment in `VkRenderingInfo`, pointing at the same view as the second
- five pipelines that differ only in depth and stencil state, sharing two fragment shaders
- `depth_bias`, `stencil_attachment` and `stencil_test` on `PipelineBuilder`
- Part 4 sits outside the Assimp gate in `code/src/CMakeLists.txt`: 22 chapters build
  without Assimp now, 26 with it

## Exercises

1. Give the slab the floor's exact four vertices and run it with the bias off. Predict
   first whether it will fight, vanish, or win — then explain the result in terms of what
   `VK_COMPARE_OP_LESS` does with two equal numbers.
2. Change `depthCompareOp` on the floor and crate pipelines to `VK_COMPARE_OP_GREATER` and
   the depth clear to `0.0`. Everything should look identical. Work out why this
   arrangement — reversed-Z — has better precision, given how floats are distributed.
3. Set the outline pipeline's `depthTestEnable` back to true and find the camera angle that
   shows why it is off.
4. Move the outline pass between the two crate draws and reproduce the spill described
   above.
5. Change the crates' `depthFailOp` from `KEEP` to `REPLACE`, then move the camera so one
   crate is behind the other. What is now outlined, and why?
6. Two crates, two different reference values, two outline passes: outline one crate in
   orange and the other in cyan without adding a pipeline. What has to become dynamic
   state, and which `vkCmdSet*` call provides it?
